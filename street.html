Home as a second Rome

Walk your town as if it were worth keeping.

The future of a civilization begins on your street. The same standard you bring to Bernini applies to a courthouse, a church, a library, and a parking garage.

The main street

What does the first block teach?

Walk it once without buying anything. Note what was built to last, what was built to advertise, and what was allowed to decay. A street is a curriculum.

The civic building

Courthouse, city hall, post office

Does the entrance lift you or process you? Are the materials local and serious, or a thin skin over a box? If the seal were removed, would you still know this was a public house?

The sacred room

A parish church, temple, or mosque

Enter if you may. Stand. Notice light, proportion, silence. Even a modest building can keep a standard the shopping center has abandoned.

The library

Memory stacked in public

Is it treated as a warehouse of wifi, or as a room that still believes reading forms a person? Look at the oldest wing first.

The cemetery

How a town speaks to its dead

Names, stone, trees, neglect. A people that cannot tend graves will not tend facades. This is not morbidity. It is the most honest civic document you have.

The repair

One thing you can refuse

A vinyl window on a brick house. A plastic sign on a carved lintel. A demolition no one argued against. Beauty is defended in small, unfashionable acts.

A one-hour local journey

Choose five stops within walking distance: civic, sacred, commercial, memorial, domestic. At each, ask only three questions. What did this ask of its makers? What does it form in a passerby? What would it mean to pass it on intact?

Write the answers. That page belongs in your Inheritance.
